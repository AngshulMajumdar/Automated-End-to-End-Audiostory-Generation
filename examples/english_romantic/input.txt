After abandoning her wedding in London, translator Mira rents a room above a struggling bookshop in Barcelona’s Gràcia neighbourhood. She intends to spend one anonymous summer finishing a translation and deciding whether she ever wanted the life she nearly married into.

The bookshop belongs to Gabriel, a widowed architect who inherited it from his mother. He plans to sell the building and accept a job abroad, but discovers that the sale would evict several elderly tenants. Mira offers to organise evening readings to help him keep the shop open while he considers his options.

Their partnership begins badly. She rearranges his mother’s shelves; he dismisses her favourite novels. Yet late dinners, disastrous readings, and walks home through the Gothic Quarter gradually expose the loneliness beneath their arguments. Their first kiss comes after an ordinary grocery run, with melting ice cream between them.

When Mira’s former fiancé arrives seeking reconciliation, Gabriel assumes she will return to London. Meanwhile, she learns he has resumed negotiations with the buyer. Feeling that their intimacy has changed nothing, she leaves the bookshop and moves into a hostel.

Apart, both confront their habit of treating departure as protection. Gabriel approaches the tenants about collectively purchasing the building. Mira completes her translation and accepts that loving someone cannot substitute for choosing her own future.

Months later, she returns to Barcelona for her book launch. Gabriel attends, carrying groceries. He asks whether she has somewhere to cook. She says yes, and invites him to dinner at her apartment.
